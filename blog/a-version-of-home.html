A Version
of Home

by Alphonso Gibbs-McGlaston

October 14, 2026  ·  7 min read

Protection: Your arms are like the walls. They keep me protected from the outside world.

Warmth: Your touch is like the chimney, giving me warmth whenever I need it.

Love: Your kindness is like the kitchen. I feel your love in the amazing food we create, the memories we make, and the moments we share within it.

Peace: Your consideration is like the bed, giving me rest and peace in places I didn't even know needed healing.

When I think of home, I think of my husband. He is my home. No matter where I am in the world, as long as I am with him, I am home.

People often think an actual place is what makes a home. But I believe it is the emotions we share and the love we cultivate that create the most amazing home within us.

There always seems to be a storm brewing somewhere around us, yet we rarely feel its force. Not because the storms don't exist, but because what we have built together gives us a sense of shelter. His arms remind me that I am not facing the world alone. When he holds me, I feel his warmth. I feel safe knowing that, wherever we are and whatever we face, he is beside me.

Home is a place of comfort, freedom, and rest. It is where you can let your hair down, release your breath, and simply exist without having to explain yourself.

The world is full of pretentiousness and pretending. We wear so many masks just to make it through the day. We learn to hide certain parts of ourselves because not everyone deserves access to our most authentic selves.

"But at home, you are allowed to be naked."

And when I say naked, I mean more than taking off your clothes. I mean being mentally and emotionally exposed. It means allowing someone to see the parts of you that the rest of the world may never get to know. It means speaking without fear of being misunderstood, sharing without feeling ashamed, and breathing without feeling the need to protect every part of yourself.

It means being able to exist without a mask.

With him, I have learned that kind of freedom.

I can be vulnerable. I can be honest about who I am, what I feel, what I fear, and what I am still learning about myself. I don't have to pretend to have everything figured out. And in creating that space for one another, we have learned so much—not only about ourselves as individuals, but also about who we are together.

He has shown me that home isn't necessarily where you lay your head at night. It is where your heart can rest. It is where your spirit can breathe. It is where you can be fully seen without feeling that you have to become someone else to be loved.

So, when I say my husband is my home, I mean that he embodies the protection, warmth, love, and peace that make a house feel like one.

"He is my home, not because of where we are, but because of who I am allowed to be when I am with him."

And wherever life takes us, that is the home I carry with me.
