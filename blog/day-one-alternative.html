That's the gap diAry was built to fill.

Why People Leave Day One

Before we get into why diAry might be the right move for you, let's be honest about what's driving people to look for alternatives in the first place. The complaints we hear most often fall into three categories.

The price increased — again

Day One moved to a subscription model and has raised prices multiple times. For what is essentially a notes app with a nice design, many users feel the value equation no longer adds up — especially when the core writing experience hasn't changed dramatically.

It's still just a journal

Day One captures your thoughts beautifully. But it doesn't respond to them. There's no AI companion reading your entry and offering reflection, insight, or just a warm acknowledgment that you were heard. In 2026, journaling apps that only store text feel like half a product.

Privacy concerns with sync

Your most private thoughts live in Day One's cloud. For many users, that's a quiet discomfort that never fully goes away — especially as AI features require more of your content to be processed externally.

The Privacy Question

People searching for a Day One alternative often have privacy at the top of their list. Here's what diAry actually does — no marketing language, just facts.

Your entries are encrypted in transit and at rest using AES-256. When you use AI features, your text is sent to Anthropic's Claude API or OpenAI's Whisper API under contracts that prohibit them from training on your content. Ghost Mode blocks screenshots. ICE Cam captures unauthorized access attempts and sends you a security alert with a timestamped photo.

And if you want to journal without any third-party AI processing at all? Use text-only entries. Your content never leaves our servers.
